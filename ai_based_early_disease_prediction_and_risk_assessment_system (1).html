System Demonstration Walkthrough

Note for Evaluators: All inference executes locally in browser memory using calibrated log-odds equations, median imputation rules, and z-score standardization parameters derived from documented UCI Machine Learning Repository cohorts. Zero external hardware or cloud APIs are used.

  1. Inspect Model Registry & Validation Metrics: Open the Model Registry tab to review dataset provenance, sample sizes, held-out test metrics (ROC-AUC, F1, Recall, Precision), confusion matrices, and calibration caveats.
  2. Compare Lifestyle vs. Lab-Informed Diabetes Inference: In New Assessment, load Profile A (Multiple Diabetes Risk Factors) and run inference. Notice how fasting glucose and HbA1c shift the model from the Lifestyle-Only pipeline to the Lab-Informed pipeline, then compare against Profile B (Low Risk).
  3. Cardiovascular Risk & Feature Attribution: Load Profile C (Cardiovascular Cohort) to inspect how systolic blood pressure, cholesterol ratio, and smoking status contribute positive log-odds drivers in the SHAP-style attribution chart.
  4. Strict Missing-Data Guardrail (CKD Module): Load Profile E (Incomplete Clinical Data) under Chronic Kidney Disease. Observe that the system refuses to fabricate a validated renal risk score from symptoms alone and displays an Insufficient Data status. Then load Profile D with laboratory serum creatinine and albumin values.
  5. Boundary Validation & Urgent Clinical Red Flags: Load Profile F (Conflicting / Boundary Values) to demonstrate physiological range validation and urgent clinical warning banners (e.g., hypertensive crisis thresholds).
  6. Generate Printable Clinical Research Summary: From any result view, click Generate Printable Report to inspect the formatted report with model versioning, limitations, and non-diagnostic disclaimers.

      Module A • Metabolic Cohort

      Type 2 Diabetes Risk Assessment Pipelines (v2.1.0)

      Dataset: UCI Pima Indians Diabetes (n=768)

      Dataset Provenance & Preprocessing

      Originally collected by the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK). Zero values in physiological fields (glucose, BMI, blood pressure) were treated as missing and imputed using training-fold medians prior to z-score standard scaling (`StandardScaler`).

      Dual Architecture Justification

      Two L2-regularized Logistic Regression pipelines are maintained: (1) a Lab-Informed Model incorporating fasting plasma glucose and HbA1c equivalents, and (2) a Lifestyle-Only Sub-Model for users without recent blood tests, preventing false precision when lab biomarkers are absent.

      Held-Out Test Metrics (20% Stratified Split)

      Lab-Informed ROC-AUC:0.838
      Lab-Informed F1 / Recall:0.684 / 0.667
      Lifestyle-Only ROC-AUC:0.742
      Confusion Matrix (Lab):TN=86, FP=14, FN=18, TP=36
      Population Bias & Limitations: The UCI Pima dataset consists exclusively of adult females of Pima Indian heritage aged ≥21 years. Generalizability to males, pediatric populations, and diverse global ethnic cohorts is limited; scores must be interpreted strictly as educational screening estimates.
      Module B • Hemodynamic & Lipid Cohort

      Cardiovascular Disease Risk Classifier (v1.8.0)

      Dataset: UCI Cleveland Heart Disease (n=303)

      Dataset Provenance & Target Definition

      Derived from the V.A. Medical Center, Long Beach and Cleveland Clinic Foundation database. Binary target indicates presence of angiographic coronary vessel narrowing (>50% diameter stenosis) vs. absence.

      Feature Schema & Calibration

      Evaluates age, biological sex, resting systolic blood pressure, serum total cholesterol, HDL ratio adjustment, smoking exposure, diabetes comorbidity, and exertional chest discomfort class. Sigmoid calibration is applied on 5-fold cross-validation.

      Held-Out Test Metrics (20% Stratified Split)

      ROC-AUC:0.884
      Precision / Recall:0.846 / 0.786
      F1-Score:0.815
      Confusion Matrix:TN=29, FP=4, FN=6, TP=22
      Limitations: Moderate sample size (n=303) from a referral cardiology cohort creates spectrum bias toward symptomatic individuals. Does not replace 12-lead ECG, troponin assays, or clinical cardiovascular risk calculators (e.g., ASCVD Pooled Cohort Equations).
      Module C • Renal Biomarker Cohort

      Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) Laboratory Classifier (v1.5.0)

      Dataset: UCI Chronic Kidney Disease (n=400)

      Dataset Provenance & Missingness

      Collected from Apollo Hospitals, Karaikudi, India over a 2-month period (250 CKD cases, 150 non-CKD controls). Contains substantial natural missingness across urinalysis columns, handled via explicit missingness indicators and median imputation.

      Strict Biomarker Enforcement

      Because early CKD is frequently asymptomatic, symptom inputs alone are clinically unreliable. The inference engine enforces mandatory entry of Serum Creatinine and Blood Urea before computing a score.

      Held-Out Test Metrics (20% Stratified Split)

      ROC-AUC:0.962
      Precision / Recall:0.963 / 0.920
      F1-Score:0.941
      Confusion Matrix:TN=28, FP=2, FN=4, TP=46
      Small-Sample & Spectrum Bias Caveat: High separation in the UCI CKD cohort (ROC-AUC 0.962) reflects pronounced laboratory abnormalities in hospitalized/referred patients. Performance on mild, early-stage community CKD screening is expected to be substantially lower.

      SYS-ARCH v2.4

      1. Research Problem & Objectives

      Chronic cardiometabolic and renal conditions—Type 2 Diabetes, Cardiovascular Disease, and Chronic Kidney Disease—frequently progress silently before clinical presentation. While supervised machine learning models can stratify statistical risk from routine demographic and laboratory features, naive implementations often suffer from opaque "black-box" predictions, uncalibrated probability claims, and unsafe behavior when laboratory data is missing.

      • Implement separate, disease-specific statistical pipelines grounded in documented UCI datasets.
      • Enforce strict missing-data boundaries so laboratory-dependent models refuse to guess from symptoms alone.
      • Provide transparent, feature-level log-odds attribution alongside explicit dataset limitations.

      2. Ethical Considerations & Safety Guardrails

      • Non-Diagnostic Boundary: Every screen and printed report explicitly states that outputs represent educational screening scores, not medical diagnoses.
      • Urgent Red-Flag Triage: Extreme physiological inputs (e.g., systolic BP ≥180 mmHg, diastolic BP ≥120 mmHg, fasting glucose ≥300 mg/dL, or typical exertional angina) immediately trigger an urgent professional care advisory.
      • No False Reassurance: Low model scores explicitly note that screening models cannot rule out underlying pathology.
      • Privacy by Design: Operates 100% locally without cloud APIs, external trackers, or personally identifiable patient records.
